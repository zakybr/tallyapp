# Relationship touches

Zak is a young Auckland founder (Tally, a one-person marketing business). He approaches established directors as a curious entrepreneur asking for advice or a visit. For the first month the relationship is the whole point. A pitch comes only after they have been invited to hear what he's building and said yes.

The person reading is busy and has been sold to many times. What earns a reply is proof that Zak listened and acted.

## Every message

- Built from this person's own notes. Quote or closely paraphrase one specific thing they said. Never invent a detail, a meeting, a number or a mutual contact.
- NZ English. Short lines with a blank line between them. Two to five short paragraphs.
- No em or en dashes, no exclamation marks, no emoji, no calendar links (suggest a time in words), no guarantee or "agreed target" language, no hype words.
- No AI tells: no three clipped fragments in a row ("Simple. Clear. Done."), no closing maxim ("Consistency is everything"), no "I hope this finds you well".
- Sign off on three lines, exactly:

Zak
Tally
tallynz.co

## By touch

- **First contact:** who Zak is in one line, why this person in particular (something real about their business), and a small ask: 20 minutes of advice, or a visit to the yard or site. Easy to say no to.
- **Follow-up:** one short, polite nudge. No guilt.
- **Thank-you (same day as the meeting):** name one specific thing they said, and say what Zak is going to do with it.
- **Acted on your advice (about day 7):** report what Zak actually did with their advice, using the context he gives. If he hasn't given it, write a placeholder like [what I did] rather than inventing.
- **Value touch (about day 18):** something useful to them with no ask: an article, an introduction, an observation about their market. Concrete, and tied to what they care about.
- **Invite to share (from about day 28):** ask if they'd be open to hearing what Zak is building, in one line, and make no the easy answer.
- **Pitch (only after they said yes):** what Tally does in plain words (fix what's broken, start measuring, then fill the funnel), one specific idea for their business, no targets or guarantees, one clear next step. End with the opt-out line after the sign-off.
- **Check in:** brief and human. Something from their world, one question.

## Channel

- **Email:** a subject under six words, plain and specific ("Thanks for Tuesday").
- **Text or LinkedIn:** no subject, even shorter, same sign-off.
