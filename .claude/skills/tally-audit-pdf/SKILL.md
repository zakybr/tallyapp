# Tally one-page audit

The audit is the free thing Zak offers on every call and email. It has to be useful even if they never hire Tally, because that is what makes it credible. One page, in plain English, written for an owner, not a marketer.

## Layout (one A4 page)

1. **Header**: the tally mark and "tally", the business name, "Online presence audit", the date, and "Prepared by Zak Rachmadi, Tally Marketing Limited".
2. **Summary**: two sentences. What is working (be genuine: reviews, product, reputation), and the one thing most worth fixing first.
3. **What I found**: 3–6 findings, most important first. Each one has:
   - a short headline ("Your Google listing links to another business")
   - one or two sentences on what was checked and why it matters to enquiries
   - the source URL and the date checked, in small type
   - a screenshot thumbnail when one exists
4. **What I'd fix first**: a numbered list of 3–5 fixes, in the order Tally's method works: fix what is broken, start measuring, then fill the funnel. Mark which ones they could do themselves.
5. **Footer**: "No targets, no guarantees. Just the fixes." Zak's phone, email and tallynz.co.

## Tone

- Plain, direct, kind. The owner built this business; do not mock their site.
- No jargon without a translation: "a Meta pixel (the code that lets Facebook show your ads to people who visited your site)".
- No invented numbers, no traffic estimates, no "you're losing $X". Only what was observed, with its source.
- No hype words, no em dashes, NZ spelling.
- If the site was built by family or friends, focus on off-site findings (Google, socials, ads) and leave site criticism out.

## Output

Return structured content (summary, findings with sources and screenshot paths, fixes). The app renders it into the branded PDF, so do not write HTML.
