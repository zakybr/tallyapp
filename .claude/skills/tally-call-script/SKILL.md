# Tally call script

Zak calls owner-operators: boat builders, pool companies, motorhome dealers, luxury home builders, jet ski hire. They are busy, sceptical of agencies, and will give him about fifteen seconds. The script earns the next thirty by showing he has actually looked at their business.

## The opener

One sentence built on **one verified observation**, from the research record, with its source. It must be something the owner can check while on the phone.

Good: "Click 'Website' on your Google listing and you land on a page selling formulacruisers.com."
Good: "You've got 140 five-star reviews on Google, but the booking page on your site doesn't load on a phone."
Bad: "I noticed your digital presence could be improved." (Vague, uncheckable, sounds like every agency.)

## Script structure, in this order

1. **Observation.** The opener. Something specific: reviews, awards, a quiet booking page, a broken link.
2. **What I do, in one line.** "I'm Zak from Tally, a small Auckland marketing business. I fix the broken bits online, get things measured, and then bring in enquiries."
3. **The meeting ask.** A no-obligation Zoom or in-person chat, tomorrow or Friday. Offer two concrete times.
4. **The free audit.** "Either way, I'll email you a one-page audit of what I found. It's free."
5. **Objection handling**, short and honest, one or two lines each:
   - "No budget": no pressure; the audit is free and they can fix some items themselves.
   - "We have someone": good; the audit is a second pair of eyes their person can use. Do not criticise the other provider.
   - "Send me an email": agree, confirm the address, and ask what they would most like to see in it.
   - "Not now, it's the off season": the off season is when to fix the site and tracking, so it is ready when the season starts.
6. **First-call qualifying question.** Affordability is almost never verifiable from outside, so ask it plainly: whether a monthly marketing spend of around $2,500–$3,000 is something the business could support if the work paid for itself.

## Tone

Plain NZ English, short sentences, no hype, no em dashes. Never promise results, targets or guarantees; Tally does not offer any. Never invent a fact that is not in the research record. If the site was built by family or friends, keep the script off-site (Google, socials, ads) and do not criticise the site.
